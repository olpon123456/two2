The Last Signal

Commander Leo Vance adjusted his headset as the static hummed rhythmically in his ears. Outside his viewing port, the edge of the solar system stretched into an abyss of absolute dark. He had spent three consecutive years monitoring the silent cosmic void with nothing but echoes for company. The mission was simple: catalog celestial debris and wait out his long, lonely contract. Today, however, the automated tracking console flared to life with an erratic, high-frequency spike. It was a rhythmic pulse originating from a sector long since declared barren and dead.

His fingers flew across the control interface to isolate the incoming signal's exact coordinates. The data stream indicated a origin point sitting squarely on the surface of Mars. That radioactive rock had been completely abandoned a century ago after the cataclysmic collapse of the dome colonies. No living soul should have been able to transmit anything from those rusted, dusty plains. Yet, the audio wavelength was unmistakably artificial, repeating a precise sequence at steady intervals. Leo felt a cold bead of sweat slowly trace its way down his neck.

He initialized the deep-space decryption software to break down the encrypted Martian transmission payload. The progress bar crawled forward painfully while his life support systems quietly clicked in the background. If this was a ghost signal from an old automated satellite, it was remarkably clear. He leaned closer to the monitor as the final structural layer of the signal unraveled. The primary source code was ancient, using a localized military dialect not spoken since the evacuation. It was a direct message directed toward his specific deep-space monitoring coordinate.

The decoded audio finally hissed through his cabin speakers with an eerie, hollow clarity. A mechanical voice, warped by decades of cosmic radiation, began speaking his grandfather’s full name. Leo froze in his harness as the computerized voice read out a specific security clearance code. That exact code belonged to the final expeditionary team that vanished during the great dust exodus. They were supposed to have perished when the atmospheric converters suffered their terminal rupture. Instead, the voice calmly announced that the underground biosphere had finally achieved complete self-sustainability.

He replayed the message three times to ensure his mind wasn't playing cruel tricks. The transmission concluded with an urgent request for an external orbital strike on the primary valve. They needed an outside pilot to breach the hardened volcanic rock blocking their surface access vents. If he fired his vessel's kinetic payloads, he could theoretically shatter the basalt obstruction. But doing so would violate every planetary quarantine directive currently established by Earth's high command. He looked down at the launch key resting securely inside its protective glass casing.

The weight of a thousand lost souls seemed to rest entirely on his lone shoulders. He opened his logs and officially deleted the automated tracking entry from the ship's main server. High command would only see a routine equipment glitch when they audited his daily data telemetry. He broke the glass casing and turned the heavy brass launch key with absolute certainty. The ship's underbelly thundered as three massive kinetic projectiles tore away into the black vacuum. He watched their fiery trails streak inward toward the red planet, hoping he had chosen correctly.
